## A browser your bots can use on your phone, Mac or Linux computer

On iPhone, iPad, Android, Mac (macOS 14 and later) and Linux (with WebKitGTK 4.1, 2.40 or newer),
Casama includes a small browser inside the app that your bots can use while you watch (Settings →
Device Connections → Browser on This Phone, Browser on This Mac, or Browser on This Computer). It
is ready after you sign in and agree to share chat and web page data with AI providers; you can
limit which bots may use it, choose when to be asked, or switch it off at any time.

**What is sent to us.** When a bot works in that browser, what it reads there (the page's text,
its buttons and fields, or a picture of the page) is sent to our server and to the AI model that
bot uses, so the bot can do what you asked. It becomes part of that chat, like any page a bot reads
on its own computer, and is kept and deleted with the chat. We also keep a record of each step
that changed something (opening a page, a click, typing), with the site's name, in your
workspace's audit log.

**What stays on your device.** Your sign-ins and cookies for that browser stay on your device, in a
store only that browser uses. They are not sent to our servers. Pages you browse yourself there
(for example to sign in to a site) are not sent to us. A bot never reads what is in a password,
card number or one-time code field.

**Your controls.** On a phone, a bot can use the browser only while its chat is open on screen. On
a Mac or Linux computer, it works only while the Casama window is open. When a site needs your
sign-in or blocks the bot's computer, Casama asks where to open it. You can stop a bot or take over
at any time, remove any site, switch off kinds of site, clear the browser's data, or switch the
whole feature off in Settings → Device Connections. Signing out unpairs your device automatically
(and clears its Linux browser folders). Workspace admins can switch it off for their workspace.

**What we don't do.** We don't use what a bot reads there for advertising, we don't sell it, and
we don't use it to train AI models.
